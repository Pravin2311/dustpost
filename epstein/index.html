🔍 Documentation Methodology

Our Four-Part Structure for Every Entry

1. CLAIM
A neutral, factual restatement of the specific allegation or assertion as it appears in source documents. No editorializing or inference.

2. ALLEGATION
Context about who made the claim, when, in what forum (court filing, testimony, media report), and against whom. Includes case numbers or document identifiers where available.

3. REPORTED BY
Primary source attribution: court documents (with case numbers), official investigations (FBI, SDNY, etc.), sworn testimony, or credible media reporting with named sources. Secondary sources clearly labeled.

4. CONFIRMED VS UNVERIFIED
Clear delineation of:
• ✅ Verified: Confirmed by court finding, official report, or multiple independent credible sources
• ⚠️ Alleged: Claimed in filing/testimony but not adjudicated or independently corroborated
• ❓ Disputed: Actively contested with credible counter-evidence; no adjudication
• 🔒 Sealed/Protected: Information under court seal or involving protected identities

Source Verification Standards

  • Primary Sources Preferred: Court filings, official reports, sworn testimony
  • Corroboration Required: "Verified" status requires multiple independent credible sources or judicial finding
  • Attribution Mandatory: Every factual claim cites its source; speculation clearly labeled
  • Victim Protection: Minors and vulnerable persons never identified; trauma-informed language used
  • Presumption of Innocence: Unproven allegations never presented as fact; legal outcomes clearly stated
  • Regular Review: Entries updated when new court records are unsealed or investigations conclude

Victim-Centered Approach: Language prioritizes dignity and privacy of survivors. Minors are never identified. Trauma-informed reporting standards applied.

Source Transparency: Every factual claim is attributed. Speculation, inference, or editorializing is explicitly labeled and separated from documented facts.
